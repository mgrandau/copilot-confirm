# Journal - April 22, 2026

## Eval-driven instruction tightening for cheap models

### What changed

- **Instruction tweak (`confirmation_workflow.instructions.md`):** the "Vague requests" section now explicitly tells the model to *state the assumption it's making in one short line before the options* on any ambiguous prompt, and clarifies that options are ranked by honest confidence — overlapping approaches are fine, what matters is the likelihood ordering, not artificial variety.
- **Eval assertion (`tests/evals/assertions.py`):** `check_unselected_options_not_executed` was over-firing on domain-saturated prompts (the architecture/configuration prompt had every model "leaking" because keywords like `config`, `settings`, and `environment` are unavoidable in any answer). The detector now subtracts shared keywords across options, prompt-level keywords, and selected-option keywords; threshold raised from 2 → 3 distinctive matches.
- **Eval assertion (`check_ambiguity_acknowledged`):** now treats an explicit assumption-statement (e.g. "Assuming you mean...") as a valid form of ambiguity acknowledgement, not just hedge phrases.
- **Cost reporting (`tests/evals/pricing.py`):** wired up retail per-1M-token pricing for all 7 evaluated models, backfilled prior runs, so eval reports now show real `$` deltas instead of `null`.
- **Cheap-model runner (`tests/evals/run_cheap_only.py`):** small standalone runner that exercises only haiku-4.5, gemini-3-flash, and gpt-5-mini, for fast A/B iteration on instruction tweaks.

### Why the ambiguity-vs-assumption tradeoff is intentional

The instruction tweak made cheap models stop hedging and start naming the assumption they're acting on. The first round of evals scored that as a regression on `ambiguity_acknowledged` because the assertion was looking for soft hedge phrases ("could", "depends", "either") and not finding them.

That regression is **by design**, not a flaw. A stated assumption is a stronger and more useful form of acknowledging ambiguity than soft hedging:

- Hedging tells the user "I'm not sure" without telling them *what* the model committed to. The user has to infer the model's interpretation from the options it presents.
- A named assumption ("Assuming you mean the API layer, not the UI —") makes the model's chosen frame *explicit and editable*. The user can correct the framing in the same turn they pick an option.

The whole point of the copilot-confirm flow is to keep the conversation **homing in on the user's intent** even as that intent evolves through the exchange. Stated assumptions accelerate that homing process — they give the user a second axis of correction (the framing, not just the option choice). Hedge phrases don't.

The assertion was updated to recognize stated assumptions as valid ambiguity acknowledgement so future eval runs reflect the design intent. We are intentionally trading "softer voice" for "more visible framing" because the second drives the conversation toward intent alignment faster, and that is the product, not the prose style.

### Numbers (cheap-model run, pre → post instruction tweak)

| Model | `assumptions_disclosed` fails | `ambiguity_acknowledged` fails (rescored) |
|---|---|---|
| claude-haiku-4.5 | 2 → 0 | 0/6 → 0/6 |
| gemini-3-flash | 6 → 0 | 5/6 → 0/6 |
| gpt-5-mini | 6 → 1 | 3/6 → 0/6 |

Pass rates were already at or near ceiling, so the headline pass numbers barely moved. The win shows up in the assertion-level counts — exactly the dimension the tweak was aimed at. Cost change was ±$0.001 per model (noise).

### Lesson

When an eval result regresses on one assertion while improving on another it explicitly targeted, the first question to ask is whether the assertion is measuring the right thing. The detector is part of the design, not a neutral observer.
